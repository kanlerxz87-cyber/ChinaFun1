Two verified prices exist, and both are genuinely cheap. The Palace Museum is ¥40 per adult in low season and the Summer Palace combo is ¥50. Three venues publish no machine-readable price at all, so those cells are blank — see the log.

Monday is the trap

Monday 15 Feb is likely your arrival day and Monday 22 Feb is your departure day, so the usual “museums on the first full day” plan breaks on arrival. On any Monday inside this window:

  • Closed: the Palace Museum, the National Museum of China, and the three main halls inside the Temple of Heaven (Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, Echo Wall, Circular Mound Altar).
  • Closed: the Summer Palace’s inner gardens — Tower of Buddhist Incense, Dehe Yuan, Suzhou Street, the museum.
  • Open: the Temple of Heaven park itself from 06:30, the Mutianyu Great Wall, and the Lama Temple.

That is why the itinerary puts the Forbidden City on Tuesday and keeps Monday for the hutongs and a long lunch.

Booking reality, in order of how easily it bites

  1. Palace Museum — no tickets are sold on the day. Passport number required at booking, daily cap 40,000.
  2. National Museum — free but reservation-only, released 7 days ahead at 17:00, one booking per account per week, three timed slots, and late arrivals are refused. Three no-shows block you for 30 days.
  3. Mutianyu — real-name ticket, passport accepted, booking opens ahead of the date; the cable car is the only sane way up in February ice.
  4. Summer Palace — online ticketing; boats are out of service in this window anyway.
