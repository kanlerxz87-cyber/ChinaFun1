What must be answered before booking

Everything that has to be answered before a booking

Seven questions, each one written so that it can be closed by a specific action rather than a discussion. Where the answer is partly known, what is known is stated — and what is missing is named.

1. Which fine-dining rooms will actually be open on our dates, and at what price? open

Why it blocks a decision: Twelve of the 33 starred rooms publish no hours at all, and none of the 33 publishes a price for two. Without a confirmed opening day and a confirmed menu price, the restaurant plan is a wish list, not a plan.

What is known today: MICHELIN lists the band only. Eight rows have hours from an official page; one row has a real menu price.

How to close it: Phone the six you care about and ask two questions: are you open on these dates, and what is the set menu price per person. Phone numbers are already in every row of the restaurants table.

2. Is a 21- or 28-day stay cheaper per night than 7 nights? open

Why it blocks a decision: The brief allows 2–4 weeks but only 7-night stays were priced, so the per-night economics of a longer trip are unknown rather than unfavourable.

What is known today: Two 7-night windows were priced. Longer-stay discounts were not tested.

How to close it: Re-run the same Booking search for 14 and 21 nights from 15 Feb and compare the nightly rate; serviced apartments in Chaoyang are the usual answer for stays over three weeks.

3. Which room type should the price comparison be based on? open

Why it blocks a decision: Two hotels priced only as twins and one only as a non-refundable apartment, so the set of comparable numbers shrank from six hotels to three.

What is known today: King rooms were priced at Hilton ($1,244), New World ($1,177) and Waldorf ($2,246); Sunworld was twin only.

How to close it: Decide whether a king is a hard requirement or a preference. If it is hard, Sunworld and the Grand Hyatt apartment drop out and the comparison stays at three hotels.

4. One base for all seven nights, or a split stay? open

Why it blocks a decision: Twenty of the 33 starred restaurants are in Chaoyang and nine in Dongcheng. A Chaoyang hotel shortens the evenings; a Dongcheng hotel shortens the sightseeing. A split fixes both and costs a lost half-day.

What is known today: All six priced hotels are in Dongcheng. No Chaoyang hotel has been priced yet.

How to close it: Price two Chaoyang 5-stars for the same dates, then compare a 4+3 split against the single-base total.

Why it blocks a decision: The restaurants are the reason for the itinerary, but 33 starred rooms in 7 nights for two people is neither physically nor financially sensible, and nobody publishes the price that would set the ceiling.

6. What is the passport and visa situation, and does it gate the bookings? open

Why it blocks a decision: The Palace Museum and the National Museum both require a passport number at booking time, days ahead. If entry documentation is unresolved those two bookings cannot be made at all.

What is known today: The U.S. advisory is Level 2 (renewed 19 Oct 2026) citing arbitrary law enforcement and exit bans. Visa-free entry rules were not verified in this pass.

How to close it: Confirm visa or visa-free eligibility for your nationality before booking any timed ticket, then book both museums exactly 7 days out.

7. Is February weather acceptable for the Mutianyu day? open

Why it blocks a decision: A full day on an exposed ridge in February can be a wasted day, and the cable car is the only realistic ascent.

What is known today: Off-season hours are published (08:00–17:30). Weather, wind-chill and cable-car suspension policy are not.

How to close it: Set a go/no-go rule in advance, for example cancel if the forecast high is below −5°C or the wind above 40 km/h, and keep that day movable.
