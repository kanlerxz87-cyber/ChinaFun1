Improvements to review before booking

Changes worth making before anything is booked

These are improvements to the plan itself, not to the data. Each one names what it costs as well as what it gains, because a suggestion that only lists benefits is a sales pitch.

1. Split the week across two neighbourhoods

Why: Seven nights in one Dongcheng hotel means roughly forty minutes each way to the Chaoyang dining cluster, twice a day. Three nights in Chaoyang after four in Dongcheng removes most of that.

What it changes: Costs one half-day of packing; buys back about two hours a day and stops the evenings being a taxi ride.

2. Book six restaurants, not sixteen

Why: Every top-end room here is a two- to three-hour tasting menu. Six in seven nights is already a commitment; sixteen is a schedule nobody enjoys, and the bill is unknowable from public sources.

What it changes: Keeps most meals in the ¥¥–¥¥¥ band and turns the trip into a holiday rather than a checklist.

3. Use free cancellation as the price hedge

Why: Every rate captured here was free-cancellation with no prepayment. Hold the room now, re-check in December, cancel if it drops.

What it changes: Costs nothing, and removes the entire “should we have waited?” question.

4. Let the Monday closures set the skeleton of the plan

Why: Arrival and departure are both Mondays, and Mondays shut the Palace Museum, the National Museum, the Temple of Heaven halls and the Summer Palace gardens — the four things people most want to see.

What it changes: The itinerary already does this. It is the highest-value structural choice in the plan.

5. Keep the Great Wall day movable until 48 hours out

Why: February wind and ice can suspend the cable car, and the wall without the cable car is a two-hour climb on ice.

What it changes: Keep Mutianyu flexible and let the two reservation-bound museum days be the fixed anchors.

6. Read the empty video column as a filter, not a verdict

Why: Only 13 of 33 starred rooms have a findable review video, and several of those are near-misses. The empty rows are mostly small hotel dining rooms that nobody films.

What it changes: Treat “no video” as “no independent footage exists”. Every 3-star and 2-star room has footage, which is where video actually helps.

7. Get the three missing prices by phone, not online

Why: King’s Joy, Chao Shang Chao and Xin Rong Ji publish no menu price anywhere, and two of them are the meals you would remember most.

What it changes: Three phone calls convert the biggest unknown in the budget into three known figures.

Method. Each value was read from the official page linked in its own row. Anything that could not be read from an official page is blank and carries a flag — it is never estimated and never copied from a blog.
