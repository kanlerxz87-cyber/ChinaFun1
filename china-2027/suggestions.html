SUG-02Route & Date TimingOptional Idea — Not Assumed

Tour Beijing FIRST (Feb 15–22) and Shanghai SECOND (Feb 22–Mar 1)

Idea: Feb 15–22 includes Lantern Festival (Sat Feb 20). Schedule major visits around verified opening days, then check current official notices for any event-related restrictions on central Beijing attractions. The sampled hotel prices and different rate plans do not establish that this is a universally cheapest stay window.

Trade-off: Beijing in mid-Feb is colder than mid-March (pack thermal layers & windproof coat), though indoor museums/subways are well heated; check local notices for any event/security restrictions closer to travel.

SUG-03Beijing Base StrategyOptional Idea — Not Assumed

Consider Splitting or Picking Chaoyang If Fine Dining Outweighs Imperial Sights

Idea: Because Chaoyang holds 20/33 (60.6%) of Beijing’s MICHELIN stars vs Dongcheng’s 9/33 (27.3%), Grand Millennium (Feb 15–22: $880.19 nonrefundable / $890 free-cancellation) or Kempinski ($968 free-cancellation) places you near the Chaoyang dining cluster; Park Plaza ($795.13 Genius/member free-cancellation) and base-Beijing ($453.20 nonrefundable / $490.20 free-cancellation) favor Wangfujing/imperial-sight access. These Booking.com totals include taxes/fees and require a live re-check.

Trade-off: Chaoyang adds ~20–30 min subway ride to Forbidden City; Dongcheng adds ~20–30 min subway ride to Chaoyang CBD/Sanlitun dinners.

SUG-06Shanghai Museum Scheduling HackOptional Idea — Not Assumed

Use Shanghai Museum East (OPEN Mondays!) + Met Jewelry Exhibition as Your Monday Arrival Anchor

Idea: While Shanghai Museum West, Yu Garden, Da Vittorio, and Sir Elly's are closed Mondays, Shanghai Museum East in Pudong (Line 2) is OPEN Mondays and hosts the Met Museum co-curated 'Jewelry: The Body Transformed' show through 2027-05-05.
