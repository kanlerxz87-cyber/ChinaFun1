Flagged irregularities

Things that did not line up. Some are errors in published sources, some are limits of the data, and some are places where a reasonable person would have quietly filled in a number. Each is stated with what it affects and how much to trust the surrounding figures.

Why this page exists. A plan built only from confirmed facts still needs a list of what was not confirmable, or the reader cannot tell the difference. Every item below produced a blank cell, an excluded row, or a caveat somewhere on the site.

Conflicts between sources

F-01 · MICHELIN says 11 two-star restaurants; its own press release says 12 conflict

The discrepancy. The live Shanghai restaurant listing on guide.michelin.com filters to 3★ 1, 2★ 11, 1★ 38 — fifty starred venues in total. The 2026 selection announcement describes twelve two-star restaurants.

What it affects. The "50-star inventory" figure on the restaurants page. The shortlist itself is built from the live count of 11, which is the more conservative read — so no restaurant here is claimed to hold a star it does not currently hold.

Most likely explanation. A closure between the announcement and now, or a venue reclassified. Neither is confirmed.

What was not done. The missing twelfth restaurant was not guessed at. That is why the page shows 11 two-star venues named, not 12.

Resolution: recount the facet on the day you book. See Q12.

F-02 · Two venues publish hours; ten do not data gap

Of the twelve shortlisted restaurants, only Fu He Hui and Yi Long Court have opening hours published on their MICHELIN Guide pages. The other ten show no hours at all, and their own websites either do not publish them or were not reachable.

What it affects. Every dinner slot on the itinerary except Fu He Hui's. They are marked TBC rather than filled with plausible times like "18:00–22:00", which any restaurant critic would tell you is the standard guess.

Why this is flagged rather than fixed: a guessed dinner time is worse than a blank one — it creates false confidence in a schedule that has not been agreed with anyone.

F-03 · No restaurant publishes a price data gap

None of the twelve publishes a menu price, on MICHELIN or on its own site. Taian Table's official site serves its current menu as a PDF with no price on it. This is unusual for a city with fifty starred restaurants and it blocks the budget entirely.

What it affects. The cost column on the restaurant table is null for every row, and the trip budget has no dinner line. The summary page says plainly that no total trip cost exists.

The one signal: Taian Table's add-on dishes at ¥218 and ¥388 — supplements, not a menu price. Recorded as such, not extrapolated upward.

F-04 · Hotel prices move with a hidden membership tier caveat

The Radisson Blu total of $922 depends on a Genius-level member rate. The list price for the same 7 nights is $1,025. A later window showed $1,013 against a list of $1,125.20.

What it affects. Every hotel total is comparative, not absolute. If you are not signed in at the right membership tier, or the tier changes, you will see the higher figure.

Handling: the dependency is recorded in data/hotels.json per property rather than hidden. Treat $922 as "achievable with a member rate", and $1,025 as the sticker price.

F-10 · Every hotel total may be missing VAT and service charge caveat

The captured Booking.com property pages note that displayed prices exclude 6.6% VAT and a 10% service charge. If that applies to the headline totals, the $922 Radisson week is really about $1,077, and every hotel figure on this site moves up by roughly the same proportion.

What it affects. Every dollar amount in the hotel tables, the price-window ladder, and the headline recommendation. It does not affect the ranking, because the surcharge is proportional.

Why it is flagged rather than fixed. The capture recorded the exclusion note but not whether Booking.com's headline figure had already absorbed it. Applying 17% on an assumption would be exactly the kind of invented number this study refuses elsewhere, so the base figures are left as captured and the caveat is published in three places — here, on the landing page table caption, and as Q8.

Resolution: open the room's price breakdown in the booking flow; the line items settle it immediately.

Chronology problem

F-05 · Your preferred window starts the day after a make-up working day scheduling

Chinese New Year 2027 is Saturday 6 February. The holiday block is 5–13 February. Sunday 14 February is a designated make-up working day — a normal working day moved to a Sunday to extend the holiday.

What it affects. Arrival on 15 February lands on the first ordinary Monday after the holiday. In practice this is excellent: prices have just dropped, crowds have gone, and the city is running normally. But it also means the Monday closures at the museum and garden are in full effect on your arrival day.

Modelled assumption: that "holiday over" means "normal hours". Yu Garden explicitly reserves the right to set Spring Festival hours by announcement, so this is assumed, not verified. See Q9.

F-06 · The museum is mid-exhibition and that changes what you see caveat

The Shanghai Museum's official visit page states that while On Top of the World Tree: Ancient Civilizations of the Americas runs (9 July 2026 – 14 November 2027), the People's Square building hosts no other exhibitions. Permanent collections are at Shanghai Museum East, 1952 Century Avenue, Pudong.

What it affects. A visitor expecting bronze and ceramics at People's Square will not find them. The itinerary schedules the People's Square building on day 2 with this caveat attached, and lists East as the alternative. This is the kind of fact that reshapes a trip and is easy to miss, because the museum is still "open".

Also flagged: the visit page gives free admission only for visitors with disabilities; general admission pricing was not shown. Left blank rather than assumed free — Chinese national museums usually are, which is exactly why assuming would have been tempting.

Method limitations

F-07 · The one-hour claim is modelled, not routed method

No live transit-routing service was reachable from this environment. Travel times are derived from straight-line distance using a declared model (6 min walk, 30 km/h rail, ×1.25 detour, +6 min for line changes beyond 4 km).

What it affects. Every minute figure on the ranking page. Individual journeys could be materially off — call it ±10 minutes.

What it does not affect. The ranking. The same model is applied to all five hotels, so relative order is far more robust than the absolute numbers. And since all five clear one hour for all five restaurants by a wide margin, the conclusion "the one-hour rule does not bind" survives a large margin of error.

The model also has a floor: a restaurant 0.32 km away still scores 7 minutes. Disclosed on the ranking page.

F-08 · Only 5 of 50 starred restaurants could be located method

Coordinates could be extracted for five venues only: Taian Table, Bombana, Fu He Hui, T'ang Court, Ji Pin Court. The other forty-five had no machine-readable coordinate on a primary source.

What it affects. "Stars reachable" is measured across five, not fifty. The figure "11 stars" means eleven among the five located restaurants, not eleven out of Shanghai's fifty.

Workaround attempted and abandoned. Geocoding the venues by name was tried four times and returned wrong cities each time (a Hongqiao address, a Minhang address, a Hong Kong venue, and a Singapore one). Abandoned as a method rather than mined for coincidences. Details below.

F-09 · Two hotels could not be geocoded and were excluded method

Pullman Shanghai Jing'an — geocoding returned a district centroid, not a building. A centroid would have placed it in a plausible spot and produced a confident but fabricated ranking. Capella Shanghai — same problem.

What it affects. The ranking covers five hotels, not seven. Notably, the excluded Pullman is the cheapest at $708, so the ranking's omission is not neutral — it omits a serious contender. That is stated on the ranking page rather than buried here.

Nothing was inferred — here is the proof

Every blank on this site represents a fact that was looked for and not found, not one that was skipped. The rule applied throughout: if an official source does not state it, the cell says so. That is why the restaurant cost column is empty for all twelve rows, why two hotels are missing from the ranking, and why the itinerary says "TBC" seven times.
