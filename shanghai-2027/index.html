The headline finding: Chinese New Year falls immediately before your preferred dates. In 2027, Chinese New Year is Saturday 6 February, with the national holiday running Friday 5 February to Saturday 13 February, and Sunday 14 February designated a make-up working day. Your preferred window starts 15 February — the day after the holiday break ends. That has three consequences:
  • Good: you arrive after the domestic travel crush, so attractions and restaurants are calmer than they would be a week earlier.
  • Risk: many Shanghai restaurants close for staff holidays in the days around Chinese New Year, and several MICHELIN venues publish no hours at all. Reconfirm every booking directly with the restaurant before you commit money.
  • Pricing: hotel rates rise steadily from mid-February through late March in the data captured here — so the *earlier* part of your window is also the cheaper part.

Holiday dates verified at timeanddate.com — Holidays in China 2027. This is a third-party calendar, not a government publication — see the Flagged page.

The recommendation, in one paragraph

Book 15–22 February 2027 and stay at the Radisson Blu Hotel Shanghai New World ($922 for 7 nights, queen bed, private bathroom). It is the cheapest hotel in this study that clears both of your hard requirements, it sits a one-minute walk from People's Square station on three metro lines, and all five MICHELIN restaurants whose coordinates could be independently verified are reachable from it within the one-hour budget — the 3★ Taian Table in a modelled 26 minutes, and four 2★ tables in 12–25 minutes.

Caveat, stated plainly: the Radisson ranks second on pure cluster density behind The Langham Xintiandi, which cannot be priced for a 7-night stay. If you are willing to pay about 40% more and tolerate a non-refundable rate, The Langham is the better dining base. That trade-off is laid out on the Ranking page and is the first open question on the Open Questions page.

What we still cannot answer

The brief asked for a clear list of questions that must be resolved before any decision is reasonable. The three that block a final booking are:

  1. Restaurant opening hours are largely unpublished. MICHELIN publishes hours for Fu He Hui only among the shortlisted venues. Taian Table, Bao Li Xuan, Tou Zao, T'ang Court and Ji Pin Court publish none. Chinese New Year closures compound this. No dinner can be scheduled until these are confirmed by phone.
  2. MICHELIN menu prices are not published for any shortlisted restaurant. The brief asked for a cost estimate per couple. That field is left blank and flagged rather than guessed — a fabricated price is worse than a gap.
  3. The 1-hour travel rule is modelled, not routed. Straight-line distance between verified coordinates is converted to a transit time using declared assumptions. No live transit engine was available. Treat every minute figure as ±10 minutes.
