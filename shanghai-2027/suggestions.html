Suggestions for your review

Changes and additions that were not implemented, because they are judgement calls rather than facts. Each says what it would cost you in money or effort, what it would get you, and what happens if you ignore it. Nothing on this page has been acted on.

How to use this page. Pick the ones you want and say so — decisions 1, 2 and 3 are the high-value ones. Everything after that is polish. None of these are required for the trip to work; the plan on the other pages stands on its own.

Highest value

1 · Split the stay to get the Langham recommended

What. Four nights at The Langham, Xintiandi (the #1 ranked base, with T'ang Court 2★ in the building) plus three nights at the Radisson Blu or Pullman Jing'an.

Why. The Langham has no 7-night availability on your dates but does have 4- and 6-night options at $932.96 and $1,176. A split stay converts "best hotel, unavailable" into "best hotel, half the week" — and you get two neighbourhoods instead of one.

Cost. Roughly $1,200–$1,500 for the hotel week rather than $922.

Downside. Packing mid-trip, and one more check-in. For a couple's week, some people would rather unpack once.

Decision needed: is a split stay acceptable?

2 · Phone the eight restaurants before booking the hotel recommended

What. Work down the call list on the itinerary page and get, for each: opening days, seating times, menu price, and Chinese New Year closure.

Why. The restaurant hours are the largest hole in this whole plan — twelve shortlisted venues, only two publish hours, none publish prices. Until that is closed, the itinerary is a skeleton and the budget has no dinner line at all. It also protects the $922 hotel booking: if Taian Table is closed 17–21 February, you may want different dates.

Cost. One afternoon of phone calls, or an email round.

Downside. None. This is the highest information-per-effort action available.

Decision needed: who makes the calls, and when.

3 · Re-run the price check across the full month recommended

What. One Booking.com calendar search for the Radisson across 15 Feb – 23 Mar, rather than the five sample points used here.

Why. The five sampled windows rise monotonically from $922 to $1,440, which strongly suggests earlier is cheaper — but five points is not a season. A dip below $922 somewhere in the window would change the recommendation.

Cost. Ten minutes.

Downside. Likely confirms what is already known. Worth it because it is cheap.

Decision needed: whether the current evidence is enough.

Worth considering

4 · Add a second 3★ alternative if Taian Table falls through

Shanghai has exactly one three-star restaurant, so there is no substitute at that tier. The suggestion instead: pre-select a backup 2★ with room to spare — 8 ½ Otto e Mezzo Bombana, which is both central and the closest starred table to three of the five hotels. Ask about it in the same phone call rather than as a separate task.

5 · Take the Pullman seriously at $708

It is $214 cheaper than the recommendation and scores 8.9. It was excluded from the proximity ranking only because its coordinates could not be verified — not because it is badly located. Jing'an is a central district. Once you confirm the address (Q6), it may well deserve the booking on pure value. The only reason it is not the headline recommendation is that the brief prioritised MICHELIN access, and that could not be computed for it.

6 · Swap the Americas exhibition for Shanghai Museum East

The People's Square building shows only the Americas exhibition until 14 Nov 2027 — the permanent bronzes, ceramics and calligraphy are all at East in Pudong. If the permanent collection matters more to you than convenience, plan East as a half-day instead, and treat the People's Square visit as optional. The current itinerary assumes convenience wins.

7 · Add a verified-hours walking route

The Bund row has no official source and is flagged accordingly. A better approach for a photography-minded couple: pick two or three verified anchor points (Yu Garden, the Bund, Xintiandi) and let the route between them be flexible rather than scheduled. Avoids inventing an itinerary for a promenade nobody operates.

Process suggestions

8 · Two review passes, not one

Read the executive summary and the restaurants page first — those carry the recommendation and the star-vs-Guide distinction. Then, separately, read the flagged irregularities page. Mixing "here is the plan" with "here is what I could not verify" in one reading tends to make the caveats feel like noise. They are not noise; they are the difference between a plan you can trust and one you cannot.

9 · Treat every restaurant dinner as provisional until confirmed

The itinerary places seven restaurant evenings. Only two of them (Fu He Hui, and Yi Long Court if used) rest on published hours. That ratio is worth holding in mind: the plan is sound in structure but soft in detail, and the soft part is fixable only by phone.

10 · Book the hotel only after the calls

Sequence matters. Hotel rates here are for specific dates, and a change of dates changes the price materially ($922 → $1,191 for a one-week shift). Confirm the restaurant calendar first, then lock the hotel. Doing it the other way risks paying for dates that do not work.

That said — if the $922 rate is scarce, the trade-off flips. Refundable rates exist at the Waldorf ($2,951 vs $2,361 non-refundable), so flexibility has a price and it is visible.
